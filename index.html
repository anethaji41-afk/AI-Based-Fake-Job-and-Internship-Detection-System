CRITICAL WARNING PATTERNS

Common Student Traps to Watch For

CRITICAL RISK

"Registration Fee" or "Laptop Deposit"

The recruiter requests money for onboarding equipment, background verification, or training material. Real employers NEVER charge candidates.

HIGH RISK

Interviews on Telegram / WhatsApp Only

Scammers avoid official company video tools (Teams, Zoom, Google Meet) and send questionnaires or text-only interviews on messaging platforms.

MEDIUM RISK

Free Webmail for "Fortune 500" Recruiters

An email like google.recruitment.team@gmail.com or infosys.careers@hotmail.com is an immediate indicator of impersonation.

Student Job Scam Academy

Learn to independently spot fake internships, recruiter impersonations, and financial traps.

💸

1. The Upfront Fee Scam

Scammers promise a high stipend or immediate job offer but insist you pay for a "software license," "registration ID," "training kit," or "background check."

Rule of thumb: Legitimate companies absorb all training, software, and hardware costs. If you pay to work, it is a scam.
📱

2. The Telegram Task Scam

Presents as "part-time data entry," "product liking," or "hotel rating." They pay small initial amounts (₹150 to ₹500) to build trust, then demand a deposit of ₹5,000+ to "unlock withdrawal."

Rule of thumb: No legitimate enterprise uses Telegram or WhatsApp to disburse daily commissions.
🎭

3. Brand Impersonation

Scammers copy logos, executive names, and job descriptions from Google, Microsoft, Amazon, or TCS. However, they use spoofed emails (e.g. careers-microsoft@gmail.com or tcs-hiring.top).

Rule of thumb: Always cross-check the job ID on the official company careers portal (e.g. careers.google.com).
📄

4. Identity & Banking Harvesting

Demands clear scans of your passport, driver's license, Aadhaar card, or bank account statements *before* an interview has even taken place.

Rule of thumb: Tax and identity documents are only collected *after* a formal signed contract is in place.
